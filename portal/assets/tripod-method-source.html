The Tripod Method

A meaning-first, fully oral-to-oral Bible translation process — with AI supporting every stage and without replacing anyone on the team. Here's what's working today, what's in testing, and what's in active research.

The short version

The Tripod Method lets a community translate Scripture orally from start to finish — no text in the target language, no bridge-language text, no writing system needed at all. The team composes by voice, guided by a conversational AI that works from a consultant-approved Meaning Map of the source text. Even consultant checking happens orally.

Where we stand: the foundations are operational — Meaning Maps are being compiled and approved, communities in 17 languages have collected over 1,500 hours through the Oral Collector app, and Acoustography and the Beads technique — the layer that makes audio editable — are working. Inside Tripod Studio, the integrated translation environment, OBT Internalize is operational, while OBT Refine and OBT Verify are in prototyping and testing. And CADA, the component designed to let a mature archive propose drafts in the community's own voice, is in active research with the Terena Pilot.

A Meaning Map is made once — and it serves every language.

A map describes the biblical passage itself — its structure, scenes, participants, tone, key concepts — and contains no target-language material. Once consultants approve a passage's map, that work never has to be repeated: the same map serves the team in every language, from the first project to the thousandth. And its reach goes beyond the Tripod Method — any translation project, whatever its workflow, can draw on Meaning Maps to enrich its FIA process (familiarization, internalization, articulation) or to strengthen its quality assurance.

Three legs, three human roles

Every traditional oral Bible translation project already stands on three human roles. Each leg of the method extends one of them — AI replaces none of the roles, it strengthens each one. And like any tripod, the process only stands when all three bear weight.

The Exegete

Knowledge of the text

The consultant or translation advisor who brings biblical expertise. Human-guided AI produces a Meaning Map for each passage — structure, scenes, participants, tone, key concepts — reviewed and approved by linguists and consultants before field use.

Produces → the Meaning Map — made once, serving all languages
The Performer

The voice of the community

The native speaker who carries the language and culture. Each community builds a Community Oral Archive of its own stories, speech, and songs — the source of the system's acoustic alphabet and a searchable reference of authentic speech.

Produces → the beads
The Facilitator

The connection between them

The team member who joins the exegete's knowledge to the performer's skill. Tripod Studio supports this role through internalization, refining, and review — with a documented trail showing the voice faithfully carries the meaning.

Produces → the approved passage

Inside Tripod Studio

Internalize

A conversational AI, constrained by the Meaning Map, guides the team through the familiar FIA sequence — familiarization, internalization, articulation — from first hearing to first recorded draft, with no source-language text anywhere.

Inside Tripod Studio

Refine

The draft is segmented into beads the team can search, edit, and rearrange — revision practices normally available only in writing, done entirely by ear. And this is not clip-splicing in a conventional audio editor, where a patched-in phrase never quite blends: after every edit the beads are reprocessed, so the melody of the speech is corrected and the seams disappear.

Meaning Maps Compiler

Operational

Specialized AI agents draft, cross-check, and revise each map; a human expert guides the analysis and makes every interpretive decision. No map ships without passing automated validation gates and human review. Each passage is mapped once — the approved map is then reused across all languages.

Meaning Map Portal

Operational

A public site where approved maps are displayed and open for external review — the exegetical layer of the method, transparent to consultants, scholars, and partners.

Oral Collector

Operational

The archive-building app, live on both the App Store and Google Play. Teams have collected over 1,500 hours across 17 languages — including 1,000+ hours of oral stories just received from the Jarawara of Brazil, a single community's archive now ten times the size CADA research needs.

Acoustography

Operational

The technique of generating a stable sound-alphabet from raw audio — a self-supervised model listens to the community's recordings and derives an acoustic alphabet, a sound-based representation of the language learned entirely from its own speech, with no writing involved — and the way back: from that alphabet to audio that native speakers recognize as accurate and natural. Acoustography has already been demonstrated in Sateré-Mawé and Terena with encouraging results, and a rigorous blind study is in development to put the evidence on public record.

Beads technique

Operational

The method that converts the sound-alphabet units into visual clusters of stable sound-unit representations — beads — which share the capabilities of a written text. Written in beads, a recording can be searched, cut, and rearranged by ear: the layer that makes fully oral revision possible.

A closer look at CADA

When a Community Oral Archive reaches 100+ hours of audio classified by genre, register and theme, it becomes feasible to train a small model exclusively on that community's own recordings — and CADA uses that model to propose draft material for the team's consideration. Because it learns only from the community's voice, it introduces no outside voice: the team still composes, decides, and approves everything.

This is active research with a live field test: we are already working with the Terena, a tonal language of Brazil — exactly the kind of language where an approach built on sound rather than text matters most. The rest of the pipeline doesn't wait on CADA; as Community Oral Archives mature, we expect it to become a powerful drafting aid across the whole method.
